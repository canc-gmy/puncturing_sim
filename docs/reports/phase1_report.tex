\begin{abstract}
This research report presents a high-fidelity explicit finite element investigation into the localized shear puncture behavior of a carbon fiber reinforced polymer (CFRP) / aluminium honeycomb sandwich panel subjected to quasi-static indentation by a flat-ended cylindrical punch ($\varnothing 25$\,mm) resting over a circular aperture ($\varnothing 30$\,mm). Formulated in the open-source explicit solver \textbf{OpenCourant}, the numerical model reproduces the progressive failure mechanism, including initial top-skin shear breakthrough, core crushing plateau, and ultimate perforation. Incorporating a polar-structured mesh aligned with the $2.5$\,mm clearance zone, 7 individual solid plies per composite skin with Chang-Chang progressive damage (\texttt{/FAIL/CHANG}), and orthotropic solid honeycomb crush formulation (\texttt{/MAT/LAW28}), the simulation achieves rigorous quantitative validation against experimental benchmarks with an absorbed energy of $221.50$\,J ($-1.8\%$ relative error against physical test $225.56$\,J) under quasi-static $100$\,mm/s smooth-ramp loading with 24 OpenMP worker threads.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Composite sandwich panels incorporating carbon-fiber face sheets and honeycomb cores are key structural elements in automotive monocoques and aerospace impact structures. Under tight aperture support conditions (where physical clearance is only $2.5$\,mm around the perimeter of a $\varnothing 25$\,mm indenter), global panel bending is heavily suppressed, and deformation is dominated by high-gradient through-thickness shear plugging, core cell-wall crushing, and localized perforation.

\section{Finite Element Model Architecture (v2.1)}
The 3D explicit dynamic puncture simulation is implemented in OpenCourant:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Polar Structured Discretization:} A specialized 2D polar-to-Cartesian transfinite mesh is generated with concentric radial rings exactly aligned at $r = 12.5$\,mm (punch perimeter) and $r = 15.0$\,mm (aperture perimeter), providing 5 radial subdivisions across the $2.5$\,mm shear band ($\Delta r = 0.5$\,mm).
    \item \textbf{Through-Thickness Stratification:} 24 discrete element layers through thickness ($7 \times 0.333$\,mm bottom skin + $10 \times 1.5$\,mm core + $7 \times 0.333$\,mm top skin), totaling 13,824 solid brick elements and 5,875 sandwich nodes.
    \item \textbf{Indenter \& Kinematics:} The $\varnothing 25$\,mm cylindrical indenter is modeled as a rigid shell body (\texttt{/RBODY}) with initial clearance $0.20$\,mm above the top skin surface ($z_{\text{face}} = 20.362$\,mm), moving at a controlled quasi-static rate $v_z = 100$\,mm/s ($0.1$\,m/s) with a smooth sinusoidal acceleration ramp over $10$\,ms.
    \item \textbf{Contact Formulation:} Penalty contact (\texttt{/INTER/TYPE7}) with Coulomb friction $\mu = 0.25$, automatic stiffness recalculation, and initial penetration clearance confirmed with 0 initial penetrations.
    \item \textbf{High-Performance Computing:} Executed using 24 OpenMP worker threads with strict mass conservation ($0.00\%$ mass error).
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
The OpenCourant finite element framework successfully reproduces the localized shear puncture kinematics of the composite sandwich panel. The Phase 1 homogenized core formulation provides an exceptional absorbed energy match of $221.50$\,J ($-1.8\%$ error relative to the experimental $225.56$\,J) and captures the initial top-skin shear breakthrough at $2.00$\,mm within $5.4\%$. Element erosion under the Chang-Chang failure criterion captures the full perforation sequence across the entire $16.0$\,mm stroke with zero mass addition and robust numerical stability. All solver decks, python automation scripts, and VTK datasets are fully version-controlled.
